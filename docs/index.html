The gap this is built on

On 26 August 2026 a combined rock-and-ice slope failure on Langtang Lirung, Nepal, flooded the Bhote Koshi and destroyed the Gyirong border crossing. We asked how often a spaceborne imaging spectrometer had actually looked at the slope that failed.

So the retrieval was built and validated on the cryosphere data that does exist — Arctic sea ice in melt, at Sirmilik National Park, Nunavut. That absence is the argument, and the prize is what can fix it.

The penalty, measured

Claims about spectral resolution are cheap, so we ran the experiment: degrade Tanager to another sensor's bands and retrain, holding scenes, forward model, architecture, schedule and seeds fixed. Noise propagation favours the degraded sensor, so every number below is a lower bound.

Against EMIT and PRISMA — neither of which can acquire this latitude — the same protocol on their published band sets leaves retrieval skill statistically unchanged: RMSE ratios 0.95–1.03 across all six parameters. The cliff sits between multispectral and imaging spectroscopy, not between 7.4 and 5 nm sampling. The Arctic gap is orbital, not spectral — and Tanager is the spectrometer that can reach it.

The check that caught a real error

An earlier version of this model scored better on every synthetic test — pond fraction RMSE 0.0030, R² = 1.000 throughout. It was wrong, and no synthetic test could have shown it. Sentinel-2C imaged this scene 29 minutes after Tanager, on the same grid; the first comparison came back anti-correlated at r = −0.68. The cause was a domain gap: the forward model reproduces real sea-ice reflectance only to about 0.03, while the network had been trained under noise up to 0.005. Retraining with smooth, physically-shaped perturbations flipped the sign. Synthetic scores got worse, as they should.

Point it at ice

The tool is built, validated and calibrated; the open archive has no glacier to run it on. Ten tasked scenes over glaciated hazard corridors — the Bhote Koshi above Gyirong first, then Cordillera Real, the Karakoram, the Caucasus — would convert a validated Arctic retrieval into a preconditioning baseline for terrain that is actively killing people. No agency can instrument a mountain range, but it can instrument fifty slopes. The practical use of this retrieval is choosing which fifty.
